# Practice Metronome

The practice metronome is a device-local timing instrument. Open **Music & Live > Metronome**. It works without synchronization and does not change songs, set lists, Live BPM, or Leviathan Live performance settings.

## Start and tempo

Set a tempo from 30 to 300 BPM with the slider, exact number, `-5`, `-`, `+`, or `+5`. **Tap Tempo** calculates a tempo after at least three steady taps. Choose **Start** to begin and **Stop** to release the click. The pendulum, beat lights, bar number, and beat number continue to show the pulse even during silent-bar practice.

## Beat mode

- **Meter** sets 1 through 12 beats per bar.
- **Subdivision** adds quarter, eighth, triplet, or sixteenth pulses.
- Each visible beat cycles through **Accent**, **Normal**, and **Mute** when selected.
- The notation row mirrors the current meter, subdivision, and accents.

Begin with an un-subdivided click. Add subdivisions only when they clarify the passage. Accent patterns can describe irregular groupings without changing the bar length.

## Poly mode

Enable **Polyrhythm**, then set Pulse A and Pulse B from 2 through 12. The voices use different pitches, and their colored rows illuminate independently as they move through one shared cycle. A 3:2 relationship is a useful starting point. Slow the tempo until both pulses can be heard and performed without guessing.

## Practice mode

**Tempo Trainer** changes the current tempo toward a goal by the selected amount after the selected number of bars. Small steps and several bars at each level generally provide a more useful technique exercise than rapid changes.

**Silent Bars** alternates audible and muted bars. The visual beat continues while audio is absent. Start with two sounding bars and one silent bar, then extend the silent interval as internal time improves.

## Full screen

Choose **Full Screen** for a distraction-reduced view on a music stand or tablet. The same controls and running state remain available. Close full screen to return to Music & Live.

## Audio guidance

Use the device speaker, wired output, or tested monitoring path. Bluetooth devices can add audible latency that varies by hardware. Confirm the output before a rehearsal. The metronome generates sound locally and does not use the microphone; Live BPM is the separate tool that listens to a performance and estimates its tempo.
