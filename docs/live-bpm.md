# Live BPM

Live BPM is a device-local tempo monitor for rehearsal, individual practice, and live performance. It analyzes rhythmic attacks from the microphone or calculates tempo from deliberate taps. It never records, uploads, or synchronizes microphone audio.

## Practice console

Open **Music & Live > Live BPM**.

1. Enter the intended **Target BPM**.
2. Select **Start Listening** and play a clear, repeated pulse near the device.
3. Watch the large BPM value, confidence bar, recent-history graph, and rush/drag meter.
4. Select **Start Click** when an audible reference is useful. Headphones prevent the click from becoming the microphone's strongest pulse.
5. Select **Stop Listening** when finished so another application can use the microphone.

**Tap Tempo** works without microphone access. Tap at least four steady beats. A longer consistent series improves the estimate.

## Reading the display

- The target line represents the tempo you intend to hold.
- The detected line shows recent tempo movement rather than only the current number.
- **Dragging** means the detected tempo is below target.
- **Rushing** means the detected tempo is above target.
- Green is within about 1 BPM, yellow is close, and red indicates a larger difference.
- Confidence describes consistency of the detected pulse. A low-confidence number is not authoritative.

Use **÷2** or **×2** when the musical pulse is being interpreted at half or double time. This changes the interpretation; it does not alter the target or any saved song data.

## Leviathan Live

In Chart mode, select the waveform-style **Live BPM** button to show or hide the compact panel. The current song's saved BPM is the target. Select **Start Listening** inside the panel when tracking is needed. The compact panel shows the same live value, direction, difference, confidence, and history as practice mode without replacing charts, playback, the metronome, or page-turn controls.

The tracker is advisory and never becomes the playback or Show Clock. Hiding the panel stops microphone listening. Song navigation, Bluetooth controls, ChordPro timing, synchronized lyrics, multitrack playback, audio buses, and the metronome remain independent.

## Better readings

- Put the device closer to a distinct rhythmic source.
- Avoid clipping from a monitor, speaker, or drum placed directly against the microphone.
- Sustained pads, legato passages, rubato, dense subdivisions, audience noise, and monitor spill can lower confidence.
- Use Tap Tempo when the room is too noisy for stable microphone tracking.
- Rehearse with the same device and placement intended for the performance.

Device microphones and vendor audio processing differ. Live BPM is a performance aid, not a timing authority or a substitute for listening to the ensemble.
